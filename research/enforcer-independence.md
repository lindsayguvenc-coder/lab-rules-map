# R39 Enforcer independence: scoring sheet

Draft, Nov 23, 2026. Facts only; every status is blank for you to score. Data: `enforcer-independence.json` (same shape as the map's cells, plus funding/staffing/overrule fields).

**Question:** who funds and staffs the inspector, and how can it be overruled?

**Cross-cutting:** Feb 2025 EO 'Ensuring Accountability for All Agencies' sends independent agencies' significant rules to White House OIRA review. Trump v. Slaughter (June 29, 2026, 6-3) struck the FTC's for-cause removal protection and overturned Humphrey's Executor; Trump v. Cook (5-4, same day) kept Fed Governor Cook in place on narrow procedural grounds. Applies to nuclear, grid and banking rows below.

## Clinical & bio labs

- **Funded by:** Federal CLIA is user-fee funded: lab certificate fees plus inspection fees must cover the program's costs, surveys included.
- **Staffed by:** CLIA surveys are done by CMS or a CMS agent. CAP inspections are done by volunteer peer teams of experienced lab professionals; CAP's volunteer network is about 7,750 pathologists and lab experts.
- **Overruled by:** CMS validates accreditors and approves their deeming authority, which must be renewed every 6 years (withdrawal of deemed status: see R31, not separately sourced here). CMS sits inside HHS, an executive department (not separately sourced here).

## Nuclear

- **Funded by:** NEIMA requires NRC to recover about 100% of its budget from licensee fees (10 CFR 170 service fees, 10 CFR 171 annual fees), minus excluded activities. FY2026: about $818.8M to recover through fees (final rule).
- **Staffed by:** Federal NRC staff, including resident inspectors at plants (see R02; not separately sourced here).
- **Overruled by:** Under the Feb 2025 EO, NRC draft rules go to OIRA; if NRC and OIRA disagree the President decides (per NRC to E&E News). EO 14300 (May 2025) ordered a wholesale rule revision with NRC's DOGE team. The President removed Commissioner Hanson in mid-June 2025 (sources say June 13 or 14); he did not challenge it. Slaughter may reach NRC's for-cause protection: our inference, not in a cited source, and not yet litigated for NRC.

## Aviation

- **Funded by:** The Airport and Airway Trust Fund, built from aviation excise taxes on tickets, cargo and fuel, is FAA's primary funding source, including safety inspections; general fund money adds to it.
- **Staffed by:** Mixed. FAA inspectors, plus Organization Designation Authorization: a unit of Boeing employees performs delegated FAA inspections and approvals. Renewed for 3 years from June 1, 2025. In 2020 FAA proposed $1.25M in civil penalties against Boeing over alleged undue pressure on ODA unit members.
- **Overruled by:** NTSB can amend or reverse FAA certificate actions (R31; not separately sourced here). FAA sits inside DOT, an executive department (not separately sourced here). FAA Administrator removal terms not fetched.
- **Gaps:** Trust fund vs general fund share for FY2026 not pulled.

## Banking

- **Funded by:** No appropriations. OCC: about 94% from semiannual assessments on the banks it supervises (12 U.S.C. 482); the Comptroller alone decides how funds are spent (12 U.S.C. 16). FDIC: assessments on bank deposits. Fed: its own earnings, budget set internally.
- **Staffed by:** Federal examiners. Historically, OCC examiners were paid directly by banks to shield them from appropriations pressure.
- **Overruled by:** Fed governors: the Court let Governor Cook stay for now (Trump v. Cook, 5-4, narrow); litigation continues in the lower courts. OCC and FDIC leadership: Slaughter's reasoning may apply; our inference, not in a cited source, not separately checked. Material-loss IG reviews already in R31.
- **Gaps:** The Atlanta Fed piece is from 2017; funding model unchanged per OCC 2027 documents.

## Pharma

- **Funded by:** Split. FY2026 request: $3.6B user fees vs $3.2B discretionary budget authority. PDUFA fees covered 78% of the drug review program in 2024 (7% in 1993, per RAPS). Review-time goals are negotiated with manufacturers and reauthorized by Congress every 5 years; PDUFA expires Sept 30, 2027.
- **Staffed by:** Federal FDA staff (not separately sourced here); FDA's Office of Inspections and Investigations is its sole field operations office.
- **Overruled by:** FDA sits inside HHS, an executive department. Commissioner removal terms not sourced here. Fee collection is conditioned on Congress keeping FDA's own appropriations at a floor.

## Chemical

- **Funded by:** In California, local CUPAs run the accident-prevention program (CalARP) on fees charged to the regulated businesses, plus a state surcharge per business ($370/yr CalARP as of Oct 2023).
- **Staffed by:** Local CUPA staff (each CUPA answers to a local governing body); federal EPA and OSHA staff (not separately sourced here).
- **Overruled by:** OSHA citations (including process safety) can be contested before OSHRC, a separate appropriated adjudicator (FY2026 request $14.2M) independent of OSHA and the Labor Department. EPA retains oversight of delegated programs (R31; not separately sourced here).

## Grid

- **Funded by:** NERC and the Regional Entities are funded by assessments allocated to end users and billed through load-serving entities (16 U.S.C. 824o(c)(2)(B); WECC and Texas RE budgets); FERC approves the budgets. FERC recovers 100% of its appropriation through annual charges and filing fees.
- **Staffed by:** NERC and Regional Entity staff (WECC is a 501(c)(4) nonprofit; others not sourced here). Regional boards include industry-sector representatives (e.g., ReliabilityFirst's six sector categories; not in a cited source).
- **Overruled by:** FERC approves or remands standards and penalties (R31; not separately sourced here). One law firm (Vinson and Elkins) reads Slaughter as making FERC commissioners very likely removable at will. That their for-cause wording matches the FTC's is not in a cited source.
- **Gaps:** Who sits on NERC's own board not checked.

## Drug supply chain

- **Funded by:** CA Board of Pharmacy (which regulates firms that distribute prescription drugs) is self-funded, mainly from license fees; it receives no General Fund money.
- **Staffed by:** Board inspectors are registered pharmacists. 7 of the 13 board members are licensed pharmacists; the board appoints its own executive officer.
- **Overruled by:** Boards controlled by active market participants lose antitrust immunity unless actively supervised by the state (NC Dental v. FTC, 2015). Federal FDA wholesaler standard still not final (R31; not separately sourced here).
