Feed It to the Beast: Procurement

Enterprise AI procurement the way labs already survive(ish) it

Hospitals already write vendor terms that pin reagent lots, withhold payment until validation passes, and require a written failure report within 24 hours. Here are those clauses, plus the few AI needs that labs never did, each linked to where it already exists.

Where each clause already exists

"Proposed" means we didn't find it in any contract or rule we checked. It's a gap, not an invention: labs never needed it because CLIA and CAP inspect the lab. Nothing inspects AI.

Four things worth knowing first

Law already writes some of these clauses

Hospitals that regularly use an outside blood supplier must have an agreement requiring notice within 3 calendar days when a donor's earlier blood turns out to carry risk. Nuclear procurement documents must state, when applicable, that the defect-reporting rule applies.

42 CFR 482.27 · 10 CFR Part 21

The federal government already asks this for itself

Agency AI contracts must require notice of new AI features, no training on agency data without consent, data and model portability, and ongoing testing.

OMB M-25-22 (2025), via PilieroMazza secondary

Aerospace shares failures across the industry

Contractors report serious defects to a shared registry and check it before using parts. Commercial products are carved out, which is exactly where off-the-shelf AI sits.

FAR 52.246-26

A real clinical AI purchase

In one county's AI scribe add-on, a price change gets 90 days' notice. A model change gets none.

Clackamas County / OCHIN, DAX Copilot SOW

How this was built

The base is a real hospital lab purchase: a VA chemistry analyzer statement of work that bundles instruments, reagents, quality control, middleware and service. Its clauses were mapped to their AI equivalents. Then the same question went to a blood supplier contract, a county's AI scribe purchase, aerospace supplier clauses, and the rules that govern vendors in banking, the power grid, nuclear, pharma, chemical plants and the drug supply chain.

Each clause links to the document it came from. Status is strict. A clause is "in hospital purchasing now" only if a hospital contract, solicitation work statement or hospital policy we read contains it. The VA source is a solicitation's statement of work: terms that become contract terms on award. Lines marked "contrast" show where a document we read falls short. A source tagged secondary is a summary, not the primary text.

This is a starting list for a procurement team, not legal advice and not a model contract.

Disclosure: this was researched and checked with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. Anthropic sells AI and is a signatory to the Sept 29, 2026 White House Accord. The framing is mine. Every clause links to its source so you can check it without trusting the tool or me.
